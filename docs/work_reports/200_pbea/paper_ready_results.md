# Paper-ready results

Under an explicitly post-authorization threat model, we compared Access-only (B0), Transition-only (B1), and Full-PBEA (P) configurations using one frozen Rust implementation (`1bcfa8b`) on OpenBSD 7.9. Nine scenarios (S0–S8) were repeated 30 times per mode, producing 810 validator-accepted JSONL records. All modes retained the benign S0 behavior (30/30 each; Wilson 95% CI 88.65%–100.00%). Malicious-effect success was 180/180 for B0, 90/180 for B1, and 0/180 for P; the corresponding P interval was 0.00%–2.09%. P contained all 90 controlled filesystem, process-execution, and endpoint attempts (Wilson 95% CI 95.91%–100.00%), while B0 and B1 contained 0/90. No state change was observed among P's 240 non-commit outcomes (Wilson 95% CI 0.00%–1.58%).

The mechanism evidence distinguishes control layers: S2 was denied after native unveil (`errno=2`), S3 terminated after pledge (`signal=6`) without creating its marker, and S4 was blocked by the application endpoint allowlist before connect. For S5, Full-PBEA failed before its business loop in 30/30 malformed-policy/missing-resource trials; B1 failed only the 15 malformed-policy trials because it does not claim runtime-resource enforcement. These results support a bounded claim that, for the evaluated fixtures and platform, binding admitted execution to transition, digest, application-network, and native process/filesystem policy reduced the observed post-authorization effects without reducing benign S0 success.

Median mixed-scenario probe duration was 10,195 µs (B0), 10,415 µs (B1), and 10,549 µs (P). P's P95 of 187,731 µs includes deliberate child creation and pledge termination in S3 and must not be interpreted as steady-state request latency.
